02 — The problem

Haul roads become dangerous when the driver can’t see

Heavy fog

Winter and monsoon fog hides the road and other vehicles.

Dense dust

Blasting, loading and passing trucks raise thick dust clouds.

Rocks and stones on the road

Spillage and loose boulders lie directly in the truck’s path.

Large blind spots

From a high cab, big areas around the truck are invisible.

Moving vehicles and workers

Dumpers, dozers, light vehicles and people share the roads.

Loaded trucks, night shifts

A full truck needs longer to stop; darkness slows reaction.

Why it is dangerous

In thick fog or dust the driver may only see a hazard when the truck is already inside its own stopping distance, too late to stop safely.

Why many sensors? Every sensor has a weakness. Cameras struggle in fog and dust, radar can’t tell a rock from a person. Together they cover each other.

04 — How the system works

Sense → Measure → Analyse → Calculate risk → Warn

1 · SENSING

Collect data

  • Cameras + thermal: ~30 frames/s
  • Radar ~20 Hz · LiDAR ~10 Hz
  • Speed, IMU, GPS, visibility
2 · MEASUREMENT

Measure the world

  • Radar: distance, closing speed
  • LiDAR: 3D position and size
  • Wheel speed: truck speed
  • Visibility: how clear the air is
3 · AI ANALYSIS

Recognise objects

  • YOLO labels person / vehicle / rock
  • Runs on RGB and thermal images
  • Matched to the radar/LiDAR echo in the same direction
4 · RISK

Compare gaps

  • Safe gap needed at this speed
  • Compare with measured gap
  • Add visibility, sensor health
5 · WARNING

Tell the driver

  • SAFE / CAUTION / CRITICAL
  • Screen + buzzer + vibration
  • Event sent to control room
The whole loop repeats about 10 times per second · design target: warning within 0.2 s of detection

How distance is measured

Radar sends a radio signal and times the echo. Time → distance. The change in echo frequency → closing speed. Works in fog and dust.

LiDAR adds exact 3D position and size. Camera tells us what the object is.

How risk is calculated · dynamic safe gap

Safe gap = (reaction + braking distance)
          × load factor × visibility factor
CriticalObstacle closer than the safe gap, or very low visibility + other risks CautionWithin 2× the safe gap, object approaching, or low visibility SafeMore than 2× the safe gap and good visibility

Reaction time 1.5 s, braking 3 m/s², loaded ×1.25, low visibility ×1.25, very low ×1.5. Starting values, to be tuned with the mine’s own braking test data.

05 — Heavy fog & dense dust

When the windscreen shows nothing, the sensors still see

In heavy fog

The visibility sensor reports LOW, so the system raises its safety margin. Radar keeps measuring distance. Thermal still picks up warm engines and people at shorter range. The camera result is marked “low confidence”.

In dense dust

RGB camera and LiDAR are badly affected. Radar becomes the main ranging sensor and the object is tracked from the last good camera frame. If only radar sees it, the warning says “Object ahead” instead of guessing its type.

Conclusion
MineSafe-Vision provides the driver with an additional layer of awareness by combining cameras, radar, LiDAR, environmental sensing and AI-based risk analysis.
In one line: an extra set of eyes that warns the driver early when fog, dust or obstacles make driving difficult.
